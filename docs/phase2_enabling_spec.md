# Phase 2 enabling specification

This specification completes WP 2.1 and WP 2.2 for Pivot P0 in the [research plan](../research_plan.md). Layer A supplies honest forest localization and a fixed-anchor effect. Layer B solves a doubly robust estimating equation for each intrinsic arm barycenter. The one-step estimator is an ablation unless its pilot satisfies the conditions in Section 5.

## 1. Observations and identified targets

One observation is \(O=(X,A,Y)\), where \(A\) is binary and \(Y\) is a \(p\)-by-\(p\) PSD covariance outcome. The initial regime is independent observations, consistency and a well-defined treatment, \(Y(a)\perp A\mid X\), \(e_a(X)=P(A=a\mid X)\ge\eta>0\), and \(E\operatorname{tr}Y(a)<\infty\). If \(Y\) is a sample covariance, the targets refer to that observed covariance measurement. Targets for latent covariances require a separate measurement model or asymptotic approximation. An event study with dependent observations requires separate design and variance arguments.

Let \(h(X)\ge0\) be a localization function with \(P h>0\), fixed or defined on an independent design sample. Write \(P_h f=P(hf)/P h\). For a prespecified subgroup \(h=1\{X\in G\}\). For a forest query \(x\), \(h=h_{\mathcal D,x}\) is frozen after training and weights nearby covariates. Conditional point \(x\) targets below are defined up to almost-sure equality through a regular conditional law. The \(h\)-target is the barycenter of the localized mixture of arm laws, including within-neighborhood dispersion. It generally differs from the barycenter at \(x\) and from the average of barycenters. For example, a conditional score uniformly Hölder-\(\beta\) near the pointwise root yields score bias bounded by \(L P_h\|X-x\|^\beta\). An interior root in a neighborhood of uniformly positive curvature inherits that order of target bias. Pointwise inference additionally needs shrinking localization and bias negligible at the statistical scale.

**Layer A.** For a common strict PD anchor \(B\), let \(U_B(Y)=T_B^Y-I\), \(\Psi_B(Y)=U_B(Y)B^{1/2}\), and \(\mu_a^B(X)=E[\Psi_B(Y)\mid A=a,X]\). The target is \(P_h(\mu_1^B-\mu_0^B)\) or \(\mu_1^B(x)-\mu_0^B(x)\). Its inputs are identified by conditional exchangeability. The lift is generally nonsymmetric and belongs to the fixed linear space \(\{UB^{1/2}:U=U^\top\}\). In an eigenbasis \(B=Q\operatorname{diag}(b)Q^\top\), store \((Q^\top UQ)_{jj}\sqrt{b_j}\) and \((Q^\top UQ)_{jk}\sqrt{b_j+b_k}\) for \(j<k\). This is a Frobenius isometry for the lift. At \(B=I\), it gives the square-root embedding up to the constant \(-I\) and isometric symmetric vectorization. Positive semidefinite \(Y\) suffices for finite transport from \(B\). The contrast is a difference of lifts. When displaying fitted arm covariances from Layer A, invert the vectorization arm by arm and apply the congruence \((I+U_a)B(I+U_a)\) with \(I+U_a\succeq0\). Such fitted arm matrices describe anchored lift means.

**Layer B.** For each arm define \(R_{a,h}(S)=P_h E[d_{\rm BW}^2(S,Y)\mid A=a,X]\) on the PSD cone and \(S_{a,h}=\arg\min_S R_{a,h}(S)\). Replace \(P_h\) by conditional evaluation to obtain \(S_a(x)\). The estimand is identified when the minimizer is unique. Outputs include \(S_{1,h}-S_{0,h}\), the geodesic contrast \(\mathcal L_{S_{0,h}}(T^{S_{1,h}}_{S_{0,h}}-I)\), where \(\mathcal L_S(U)=US+SU\), and differences of eigenvalues and leading spectral projectors computed separately by arm. An observed fixed weighting function and fixed anchor give propensity-invariant population targets when the law of the potential outcomes given \(X\) and the marginal covariate law are held fixed. A learned control anchor or learned partition can vary when the assignment law changes. Comparisons across assignment policies can fix the anchor and localization or target the pointwise intrinsic effects. Layer B has no anchor in its population score.

The enabling regime assumes each requested Layer B target is interior and the population score derivative is positive definite on the symmetric matrix space. Population positive-weight squared risk is convex and coercive because the trace term grows linearly and the cross term is at most a constant times \(\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}S}\). The Hessian formula below gives strict positivity at an interior target if, for every symmetric \(V\ne0\), the localized arm law has positive probability of \(F^\top V F\ne0\) for \(Y=FF^\top\). A positive probability of full-rank observations is sufficient. Rotating singular observations can meet the condition as well. The local strictly positive curvature at an interior zero gives a unique global minimizer by convexity. An interior candidate and \(E[Y]\succ0\) alone are insufficient. For example, the equal mixture of the two coordinate rank-one projectors in dimension two has risk \(\operatorname{tr}S+1-\sqrt{S_{11}}-\sqrt{S_{22}}\), with all PSD matrices having diagonal entries \(1/4\) as minimizers. Conversely, if \(B\succ0\), let \(G=2P\) with \(P\) uniform on rank-one projectors onto the two coordinate and two diagonal directions and \(Y=GBG\). Then \(E G=I\), \(T_B^Y=G\), and their supports span the symmetric directions, so \(B\) is the unique interior barycenter. Layer A and the BW metric admit singular outcomes. Layer B inference at a genuinely singular target and inference for a nonunique target are outside this regime. If a ridge is applied to the observations, report the estimand for the resulting transformed observations.

## 2. Exact BW score and derivative

Use the full squared BW loss \(\ell(S,Y)=d_{\rm BW}^2(S,Y)\). Its Euclidean gradient is \(g_S(Y)=I-T_S^Y\). For the half-squared loss the Euclidean gradient is \(g_S(Y)/2\). Under \(g_S^{\rm BW}(V,W)=\langle \mathcal L_S^{-1}(V),W\rangle_F/2\) the Riemannian gradient of the half-squared loss is \(\mathcal L_S[g_S(Y)]\). The prototype solves a root of the Euclidean score and uses a BW retraction for the Newton displacement. A Riemannian Hessian away from a stationary point includes connection terms and is a different object. [Xu and Li, Eq. (4) and Lemma 21](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume26/24-0493/24-0493.pdf), [Kroshnin, Spokoiny and Suvorikova, Appendix A.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.00226).

For \(Y\succeq0\), factor \(Y=FF^\top\) with \(F\) of full column rank, \(M=F^\top S F\), and \(R=M^{1/2}\). The trace term in the loss is \(\operatorname{tr}R\). For a symmetric perturbation \(V\), solve

\[
RL+LR=F^\top V F,\qquad
T_S^Y=FR^{-1}F^\top,\qquad
H_S(Y)[V]=-D_ST_S^Y[V]=FR^{-1}LR^{-1}F^\top .
\]

The zero outcome has \(T=0,H=0\). Differentiating \(\operatorname{tr}R\) using this Sylvester equation proves the gradient formula. In the eigenbasis of \(R\), the Hessian quadratic form is \(\sum_{ij}(F^\top VF)_{ij}^2/[r_i r_j(r_i+r_j)]\). Thus the individual Hessian is self-adjoint and positive semidefinite. For numerical stability determine the support from \(Y\) before taking the root, retain positive eigenvalues above the relative eigensolver roundoff threshold and reject material negative eigenvalues. Use the orthogonal Procrustes norm of aligned covariance factors for numerical BW distance near equality. Both implementations avoid square roots of spurious small positive eigenvalues produced by eigendecomposing a second, rank-deficient congruence. [Bhatia, Jain and Lim, trace and Procrustes characterizations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01504), [Kroshnin, Spokoiny and Suvorikova, Lemmas A.2–A.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.00226).

For the subgroup risk, \(E_h[g_S(Y(a))]\) vanishes at \(S_{a,h}\). Its derivative there is \(P_h E[H_S(Y)\mid A=a,X]\). On any compact interior set of \(S\), the score and derivative are locally controlled by functions of the covariance outcome. For estimator consistency use uniform convergence of the fitted moment on a neighborhood, plus local identification. The user-facing validity claim is conditional on this regular regime.

## 3. Double robustness is a score identity

For \(f(Y)=\Psi_B(Y)\) or \(g_S(Y)\), put \(m_{a,f}(X)=E[f(Y)\mid A=a,X]\) and use the arm score

\[
\phi_{a,f}(O;\bar e_a,\bar m_a)=
\bar m_a(X)+\frac{1\{A=a\}}{\bar e_a(X)}(f(Y)-\bar m_a(X)).
\]

Conditional on \(X\) and independent nuisance training, its mean minus \(m_{a,f}\) equals \((1-e_a/\bar e_a)(\bar m_a-m_{a,f})\). This proves exact mean double robustness at fixed \(S\) or fixed \(B\), and zero first derivatives in both nuisances at truth. Multiplication by a frozen \(h\) preserves the identity. With bounded inverse propensity the integrated remainder is bounded by the product of localized \(L^2\) nuisance errors. For Layer B, the regression function is the conditional mean of the transport score evaluated at the current \(S\). Substituting \(I-T_S^{\widehat S_a(X)}\) for this mean generally fails under random noncommuting outcomes, even at a correct conditional barycenter. The verification script exhibits a nonzero first-order derivative for that proxy.

For supported exact-root estimation, uniform convergence of the augmented score in an interior neighborhood under either correct nuisance (and bounded estimated propensity) plus local root selection and invertible population Jacobian implies consistency: any accepted near root solves the limiting identified equation, and its error is controlled by the moment error and numerical tolerance through local strong curvature. This statement does not give an arbitrary fixed one-step update the same property. Confidence intervals further require that the nuisance product, solver residual and higher-order remainder be negligible relative to sampling variability. Consistency from just one correct nuisance does not itself imply that rate. [Kennedy, two-stage DR learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14497).

## 4. Honest forest and score construction

1. Before any tuning, split anchor, design and estimation observations. A prespecified external anchor lets the anchor split be omitted. Otherwise use a single positive-weight control-arm BW barycenter from the anchor split plus a prespecified positive ridge. Condition on this common realized \(B\) and keep it fixed in the design and estimation folds. Centering, scaling, propensity tuning and any outcome-specific nuisance fit are trained outside the corresponding score fold. Use the same split IDs and final estimation observations for all comparisons.
2. In the design split compute out-of-fold Layer A scores for both arms using the common anchor. For a design-subsampled tree, maximize \(n_L n_R/(n_L+n_R)^2\left\|\bar\Gamma_L-\bar\Gamma_R\right\|_2^2\) in the isometric coordinates of Section 1, with \(\Gamma=\phi_{1,\Psi_B}-\phi_{0,\Psi_B}\), declared minimum design-leaf size, feature sampling and deterministic tie breaks. Prespecify tree count, subsampling, minimum leaf sizes and seeds at the prototype freeze. No estimation observation or its treatment/outcome affects the partition. If experimenting with per-tree anchors, use a common anchor within each tree and average only scalar split criteria or resulting weights. Pooling Layer A contrasts across different anchors changes the target and requires a common re-encoding.
3. For tree \(t\), let \(L_t(x)\) be its terminal cell and \(b_t(x)\) its positive relative occupancy among the design observations. Use \(h_{\mathcal D,x}(z)=T^{-1}\sum_t 1\{z\in L_t(x)\}/b_t(x)\), freezing both leaf membership and denominators before estimation. Set \(\alpha_i(x)=h_{\mathcal D,x}(X_i)/\sum_{j\in E}h_{\mathcal D,x}(X_j)\) on the evaluation split \(E\). For a prespecified subgroup, substitute its indicator for \(h\). Check that \(\sum_i\alpha_i=1\), that each arm has positive local mass and that localized effective sample size and propensity overlap are recorded. Return missing support when the denominator vanishes. Traditional per-tree evaluation-leaf denominators define a distinct estimator and require separate inference for random weights. The design-frozen construction retains adaptive forest neighborhoods. [Athey, Tibshirani and Wager, §2 and §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.01271).
4. In \(E\), cross-fit both arm propensities and regressions, with predictions clipped to a prespecified overlap bound (the bound must contain the true propensity support in a claim relying on exact propensity specification). For Layer B's transparent prototype, fit a constant-reproducing linear score smoother within each arm and training fold, such as ridge regression with an unpenalized intercept, training-standardized covariate main effects and squares, and validation-fixed penalty. Its row weights \(W^a_{ij}\) depend on training covariates/arm membership and tuning fixed before evaluation. For each proposed \(S\) the regression response is \(g_S(Y_j)\). Apply the same weights to \(H_S(Y_j)\) when differentiating. For Layer A evaluate the out-of-fold lift score once and pool the common coordinates with the same \(\alpha_i\). Fold-level lack of arm support must be logged. A general nonlinear score learner requires a well-defined derivative or a separate derivative estimator and does not supply exact linear smoothing weights.
5. Keep one common pilot and one pooled moment per arm/query. The pilot is a nonnegative-weight local Hájek IPW BW barycenter, computed by the positive-weight fixed-point map in Section 5. A pilot computed using \(E\) may depend on evaluation outcomes. The relevant orthogonality is that of the fitted score process at fixed \(S\), followed by consistent local root selection. Treating a same-evaluation-sample pilot as a fixed anchor for Layer A, or conditioning on it to claim independence of its scores, would be invalid.

For a constant-reproducing score smoother, the sample moment \(M_a(S)=\sum_{i\in E}\alpha_i\phi_{a,g_S}(O_i)\) has the exact form \(\sum_{j:A_j=a}\omega_{aj} g_S(Y_j)\), where

\[
q_i=1\{A_i=a\}/\widehat e_a(X_i),\qquad
\omega_{aj}=\alpha_j q_j+\sum_i \alpha_i(1-q_i)W^a_{ij}.
\]

\(W^a_{ij}=0\) when \(j\) is in the evaluation fold for unit \(i\) or is outside the arm. The signed weights sum to one, because each regression row reproduces a constant. Precompute them without materializing the full matrix when the smoother factors into design and coefficient matrices. The empirical Jacobian is \(\widehat H_a(S)=\sum_j\omega_{aj}H_S(Y_j)\). The simple positive-IPW Hessian generally estimates a different derivative under propensity misspecification. Keep original individual scores for inference, even when using the signed weight representation for solving.

## 5. Primary augmented-score solution and ablations

Compute the nonnegative-weight pilot, with \(v_j\propto\alpha_jq_j\), using \(G=\sum_j v_j T_S^{Y_j}\), \(S_{\rm new}=GSG\), from a ridge-stabilized weighted arithmetic initializer. Log convergence and the boundary/conditioning diagnostics. An interior initializer can help computationally without changing the target loss. If an initial pilot is singular, add and log a small pilot-only ridge measured relative to a prespecified covariance scale. If there is no local arm support, return a failed fit. [Bhatia, Jain and Lim, fixed-point barycenter iteration](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01504).

At each primary iteration, use the exact signed Jacobian. Seek \(D=-\widehat H_a(S)^{-1}M_a(S)\) in an orthonormal symmetric-vectorization basis and solve \(US+SU=D\). The update

\[
S_{\rm new}=(I+tU)S(I+tU)=S+tD+t^2USU
\]

has the exact ambient first variation. Accept the earliest \(t=1,1/2,\ldots,2^{-14}\) with \(\lambda_{\min}(I+tU)>10^{-6}\), a finite, numerically interior candidate, and \(\|M_a(S_{\rm new})\|_F\le(1-10^{-4}t)\|M_a(S)\|_F\). Stop successfully only when the score is below \(10^{-8}\) in prototype units and the signed Hessian's smallest eigenvalue exceeds \(10^{-10}\max(1,\lambda_{\max})\) in the current covariance units. For general inference, make the solver tolerance negligible relative to the score sampling scale. Record 30-iteration limit, any guard rejection, condition number, final score norm and statuses for unsupported, nonpositive/unstable Jacobian, stalled or nonconverged queries. On failure expose the last interior iterate and the pilot for diagnostics. Exclude them from accepted root estimates, count their failure in experiment denominators, and suppress their root-based CIs. This is a guarded local solver, so a finite-sample signed loss with another stationary point, a singular score derivative or no usable interior root can produce failure. Individual signed contributions are no longer a probability measure. For instance in \(p=1\), weights \(2,-1\) at outcomes \(1,16\) give score \(1+2/\sqrt S\) and force the signed objective minimum to the boundary. A congruence with a symmetric indefinite multiplier still preserves PSD and can fold across the injectivity region, so PSD alone cannot validate an update.

The one-step ablation starts from the same pilot, uses the exact augmented Jacobian, and takes the first Newton step, damped to maintain the interior when needed. When the pilot is close to the true root and the safeguard is inactive, the deterministic expansion removes the leading pilot error and leaves \(O(\|S_{\rm pilot}-S_{a,h}\|^2)\) with the true derivative. Local estimation adds the derivative-estimation product term and sampling error. An IPW pilot can be inconsistent with the correct score regression under persistently wrong propensity. The exact three-stratum example in the gate memo demonstrates residual bias after one step, even with the true Jacobian, and also with the original positive-IPW Hessian.

The entropy comparator minimizes KL divergence to positive IPW priors on local arm units subject to simplex constraints and balance of the selected covariate features against \(\sum_i\alpha_i f(X_i)\), then computes a nonnegative BW barycenter. Choosing this comparator is justified for a finite score model whose conditional score entries are in the span of balanced features (uniformly in \(S\) near the root), or when correct IPW priors and a regular, vanishing calibration correction give a consistent weighted law. Balance of generic finite features alone does not guarantee score double robustness with arbitrary outcome regression. A target moment outside the convex hull of local arm feature vectors makes exact balance infeasible. The stress run records bounded-dual optimization success and moment tolerance, which can fail even in a feasible problem. [Zhao and Percival, entropy balancing under specific outcome and propensity models](https://doi.org/10.1515/jci-2016-0010).

## 6. Spectral derivatives and simultaneous inference

For fixed finite target set \(h\), let \(H_{a,h}=D P_h m_{a,g_S}\) at \(S_{a,h}\). The population linear term of the regular augmented root is

\[
V_{a,h}(O)= -H_{a,h}^{-1}\frac{h(X)}{P h}\,
\phi_{a,g_{S_{a,h}}}(O;e_a,m_{a,g_{S_{a,h}}}).
\]

Since the population weighted score mean is zero at the target, the denominator influence cancels. For Layer A (an ordinary weighted mean), the contribution instead uses the arm score minus its localized target mean. These formulas are conditional on the frozen design and anchor. With a finite fixed set of subgroups or bounded frozen kernels, consistent Jacobian, bounded inverse propensity, finite second moments, root and product remainders negligible at \(n_E^{-1/2}\), and \(P h>0\), the vector root has the usual conditional i.i.d. first-order expansion. With cross-fitting the true-score leading term is i.i.d. conditional on the fixed design, and the empirical process from the estimated fold scores requires the corresponding localized mean-square consistency and product rate. For varying/shrinking kernels, require appropriate effective sample size, leverage/moment control, localization bias correction or undersmoothing relative to the standard error, and proof or calibration of the forest expansion. Ordinary adaptive weights reusing the evaluation outcomes or random evaluation-leaf denominators are outside the fixed-kernel argument. The empirical gate must test the actual fitted estimator, including nuisance fitting; a synthetic oracle-score bootstrap check cannot establish its coverage.

For a simple eigenvalue \(\lambda_j\) of \(S\) with unit eigenvector \(u_j\), \(D\lambda_j[V]=u_j^\top V u_j\). For \(1\le r<p\) with \(\lambda_r-\lambda_{r+1}=\delta_r>0\),

\[
D\Pi_r[V]=\sum_{j\le r<k}
\frac{u_k^\top V u_j}{\lambda_j-\lambda_k}
(u_k u_j^\top+u_j u_k^\top).
\]

The formula is invariant to eigenvector signs and to orthogonal basis changes within tied eigenspaces when the boundary gap stays positive. Its finite-dimensional remainder is quadratic in \(\|V\|/\delta_r\) when the perturbation is small relative to the gap. Compose with each arm's \(V_{a,h}\) and subtract derivatives for arm contrasts. The raw matrix contrast uses \(V_{1,h}-V_{0,h}\). For the intrinsic BW log contrast, solve \(E_T S_0 T+TS_0E_T=V_1-TV_0T\) by differentiating \(TS_0T=S_1\), with both arms interior, then use \(\mathcal L_{S_0}(E_T)+\mathcal L_{V_0}(T-I)\). Layer A's optional arm reconstruction uses \(D[(I+U)B(I+U)][E]=EB(I+U)+(I+U)BE\) if no projection is active. [Koltchinskii and Lounici, §2 and Lemma 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.07333).

For each accepted arm/query solve the fitted Hessian against each individual's fitted score centered by its \(\alpha\)-weighted mean and multiply by \(-\alpha_i\). Apply the above derivatives to form contributions \(v_{ik}\) for the prespecified contrasts \(k\), all on the same observation index. Compute \(s_k=(\sum_i\|v_{ik}\|_k^2)^{1/2}\) with absolute value for a scalar and Frobenius norm for a matrix. Draw one vector of independent mean-zero unit-variance multipliers per replicate, shared across arms, queries and metrics. Use the 95th percentile of \(\max_k\|\sum_i \xi_i v_{ik}\|_k/s_k\), with a prespecified seed and at least 999 draws for final simulation, to set all radii \(c_{.95}s_k\). Report zero-variance directions separately and flag any failure of support, Jacobian or guard checks. A matrix ball with center \(\widehat C\) and radius \(R\) induces the norm interval \([\max(0,\|\widehat C\|_F-R),\|\widehat C\|_F+R]\), including under a null contrast. Joint coverage uses one maximum over the whole declared family. At fixed finite dimension the supported argument is a multivariate multiplier approximation and continuous mapping. Growing-dimension versions require their own Gaussian approximation conditions. [Chernozhukov, Chetverikov and Kato, Gaussian multiplier bootstrap for vector sums](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.3661), [Kroshnin, Spokoiny and Suvorikova, BW barycenter bootstrap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12612).

Require a gap diagnostic in both arms. For example, a prespecified leading-rank target can be accompanied by a separate pilot operator-error bound and the inequality \(\widehat\delta_r>4 b_{\rm op}\). The threshold is a stability check and requires prospective calibration. A fixed \(r\) whose boundary lies inside an eigenvalue tie has no unique rank-\(r\) projector. Extend to a prespecified isolated whole cluster/gap-protected rank and classify the associated projection as a different target, or report the matrix-only region. Likewise, omit regular individual-eigenvalue bands for tied modes and optionally use a prespecified cluster sum. Include abstentions in coverage reporting for the original family. Reusing a data-selected cluster to declare unconditional coverage requires a simultaneous guarantee over candidate clusters or an independent selection split.

## 7. Implementation checks and deferred work

The [verification script](../scripts/verify_phase2_enabling.py) checks the identity anchor, radial geometry up to singular observations, the exact Euclidean and BW gradient factors, the Fréchet Hessian including at rank-deficient outcomes, the AIPW score identities, one-step pilot bias and local signed-root recovery, a 100-repetition signed-weight stress test, and the spectral derivatives. It also runs a small fixed-stratum multiplier sanity check using direct symmetric perturbations. Forest comparisons, fitted-model coverage over the preregistered grid, and the theoretical transfer to shrinking pointwise neighborhoods remain Phase 3–4 and, if earned, Phase 7 work.
